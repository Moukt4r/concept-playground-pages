Card Storybook

Fifteen card styles, rendered by six different scripts, in a pipeline where the only way to see one used to be building a video that used it. This page renders one example of each from the same code the videos use.

Why this exists. Two styles had never been exercised on a real card and failed the first time they were. Nine of eleven chart kinds had never rendered at all. An unused style is not a neutral fact — it is a defect waiting for the video that finally needs it.

What constrains a card

Rules these cards obey

Size encodes magnitude — never font size

Bar length or colour carries the quantity. A card once shipped with mathematically correct bar ratios where the longest bar was 65px, because wide labels stole the width: proportionally right, visually dead.

layout_gate L12 now enforces absolute floors — top bar ≥200px, spread ≥40px — on top of the ratio check.

Parts must sum to the total

The ledger styles refuse to render when the blocks do not add up to the printed total. A breakdown whose parts disagree with its own headline is a false claim, not a styling defect — and the viewer can only catch it with a calculator.

Minutes are the sample, not a metric

Comparison cards print minutes and starts under each name. points_per_game divides by appearances, so twenty minutes off the bench costs a whole game of denominator — two players can share a rate on completely different exposure.

It is a caption rather than a compared row on purpose: a bar for “2721 vs 1920” would invite reading more minutes as better.

The badge is the current club

A player who moved in the summer flies his new badge, and the move is stated on the card. Numbers earned elsewhere are labelled “another club” — a caveated figure is honest, an uncaveated one is a claim about a shirt he never played in.

Text stays inside the safe area

The Shorts button rail owns everything past x=950, so the safe area is asymmetric. Columns centred on raw frame width drift outward on the right — a defect that hid until a value happened to be wide enough to cross the line.

Photos are free-licence only, here

The pipeline can use official Premier League cutouts inside a video under a credit stamp. This page is public, so it uses only CC or public-domain portraits, each with its photographer named. Nine restricted images in the same cache are excluded by licence, not by name.
